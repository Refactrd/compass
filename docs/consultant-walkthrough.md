# Using Compass

Compass is Refactrd's internal consulting assistant. It is not a general chatbot: it
holds Refactrd's own reasoning discipline (organization, function, workflow,
bottleneck, evidence, impact, intervention, adoption, outcome) and a maturity ladder
for recommending interventions (clarify, improve, assist, integrate, operationalize,
transform), and it applies both to your actual client situations rather than jumping
straight from a stated problem to a solution.

This guide covers everything you need for your first session.

## Getting started

You will receive an email invite from Compass. Open it and set a password. That
signs you in and takes you straight to your workspace.

If your invite link has expired or was not delivered, ask your admin. They can send
a new one or hand you a one time sign-in link directly.

Forgot your password later. Use "Forgot password" on the sign-in page. You will get
a reset link by email, valid for an hour.

## Starting a conversation

There is no form to fill in. Just describe the situation in your own words, the way
you would explain it to a colleague:

> Northwind Logistics is a mid-size freight brokerage. Their dispatch team is still
> coordinating loads over phone and spreadsheet, and it is starting to cost them
> missed pickups.

If you are not sure where to start, the workspace shows a few starting point prompts
when a conversation is empty. Click one to drop it into the message box and edit it
before sending, or just send it as is.

## While Compass is working

Every answer streams in and passes through three stages, shown live rather than a
generic spinner:

1. **Looking through the knowledge base** — retrieving anything relevant from
   Refactrd's own material.
2. **Working through the reasoning chain** — applying the methodology to what it
   found and what you have said so far.
3. **Writing the answer** — the response itself starts appearing.

A response can take a little while on a substantive question. That is the reasoning
discipline actually running, not a stall.

## Reading an answer

Every response ends with one plain sentence about how confident Compass is in it.
This is deliberate: not a score, not a percentage. If there is not enough
information yet to say something with confidence, it says so directly rather than
filling the gap with a guess.

Below that, look for **sources**:

- If Compass drew on Refactrd material, you will see a count like "3 sources."
  Click it to expand the list of document titles.
- If nothing in the knowledge base matched, it says so plainly: "No Refactrd
  material matched this question. Answered from methodology alone." That is not an
  error. It means the answer is Compass reasoning from the methodology itself
  rather than from a specific document, and you should treat it accordingly.

If you ask for a specific format ("put this in a table," "write this as an email to
the client," "give me a decision memo"), Compass matches it. Otherwise it answers in
plain conversational prose.

After an answer, you may see a row of **follow-up questions** as clickable chips.
These come from the same methodology, not a separate feature: they are whatever the
reasoning chain says is the next useful thing to ask. Click one to send it, or ignore
them and ask your own.

## The client context panel

On the right side of the workspace is **Client context**. You do not fill this in
yourself. As you describe a situation, Compass extracts the client's name, industry,
size, and a running notes field, and fills the panel in automatically.

Everything in the panel is editable. Click into any field and correct it, if Compass
got something wrong or you want to add detail by hand. Your edit sticks: Compass will
not silently overwrite a field you have corrected on the next turn.

**If you mention a company Compass already has a record for** (or something close to
one), it will not merge automatically. Instead you will see a prompt in the panel
showing both names side by side, asking you to confirm whether this is the same
client or a different one. Choose deliberately: a wrong merge mixes two engagements'
context together and is not easily undone.

Client records are shared. Any consultant can see and edit any client, on purpose:
if a colleague already has context on an organization you are now working with, you
benefit from what they entered, and vice versa.

## Managing conversations

The left sidebar lists your own conversations, most recent first. Yours only:
another consultant cannot see or open a conversation you started, and you cannot see
theirs.

- **New** starts a fresh conversation.
- **Search** filters your conversation list, matching on title and the linked
  client.
- Each row has a delete action for conversations you no longer need. Deletion is
  permanent.

## Your daily limit

Each consultant gets 20 questions a day, reset at UTC midnight. A counter at the
bottom of the workspace shows where you stand ("14 of 20 questions today"). If you
hit the limit, Compass tells you plainly and shows when it resets rather than
letting you send a question that quietly goes nowhere.

## If something goes wrong

- **Compass cannot reach the AI provider**: your message is saved either way, and
  you will see a retry option. Nothing is lost.
- **Your account gets disabled mid-session**: you will be redirected to a plain
  explanation screen. Contact your admin.
- **The daily limit is reached mid-conversation**: you will see the reset time,
  clearly, not a vague error.

None of these should leave you guessing. If you hit something that does not explain
itself, tell your admin exactly what you were doing when it happened, that detail is
what makes it fixable.

## A good first session

A useful way to spend your first few questions:

1. Describe a real client situation you are currently working, in your own words,
   the way you would explain it to a colleague.
2. Check the context panel. Correct anything it got wrong.
3. Ask a specific follow-up, or click one of the suggested ones.
4. Ask for the answer in a format you would actually use, a table, an email draft,
   a short memo, and see how it adapts.
5. Check the sources on an answer that should be grounded in Refactrd material, and
   one that probably is not, and notice the difference in how each is presented.

That is enough to get a real feel for how Compass reasons, corrects itself, and
tells you what it does and does not know.
